Q1. Who are you and what do you do?

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation constituted in 2016, operating in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of 41,000 people in northern England. We employ six staff (4.2 full-time equivalent), supported by 25 regular volunteers, with annual expenditure of £214,000 in our last filed accounts. Our work supports young people aged 11 to 19 and local families across two specific neighbourhoods on the east side of Kelverton: the Marlpit ward on the east bank of the river, and Ferry Bank, an estate situated north of the old goods yard.

In Marlpit, where 38 per cent of households sat in the lowest income decile at the 2021 census, we run open-access youth sessions three evenings a week, a weekly supper club at St Aidan's Parish Hall, and a small family advice desk. In Ferry Bank, we opened a second delivery site in October 2024, engaging 31 young people in its first term. Youth delivery is led by Priya Raval, our delivery coordinator since 2019, who holds a level 3 youth work qualification and runs four weekly sessions in Marlpit. Our board of trustees is chaired by Delroy Ferguson, a retired further education lecturer and Ferry Bank resident since 2018.

We connect young people directly to local institutions. Since 2023 we have referred school-leavers to Dunmore Sixth Form College, located one mile from our Marlpit site, where 27 young people were referred and 18 enrolled in the 2024–25 academic year. We also receive referrals from Barrowfield Youth Justice Team, which directed nine young people to our provision between April 2024 and March 2025, six of whom live in Ferry Bank. In early 2025, we piloted Second Chances, a 12-week progression course in Marlpit: 19 of 24 starters completed, and 11 progressed into college places, apprenticeships, or paid employment. Alongside youth work, our adult cook-and-eat course, Night Kitchen, ran twice in Ferry Bank in 2025, graduating 22 adults, 15 of whom became regular supper club volunteers.

Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

The acute issue across Marlpit and Ferry Bank is the structural drop-off between informal neighbourhood engagement and formal progression into post-16 education, vocational training, or employment for vulnerable school-leavers.

Our evidence stems from direct delivery data across both neighbourhoods. In Marlpit, 38 per cent of households are in the lowest income decile. This financial strain directly impedes post-16 transitions; while 27 young people were referred to Dunmore Sixth Form College in 2024–25, nine did not convert referral into enrollment, dropping out during the summer transition window due to lack of sustained, structured guidance. In Ferry Bank, youth detachment is compounded by geographic separation north of the goods yard and contact with statutory systems; Barrowfield Youth Justice Team referred nine young people to us in 2024–25 (six from Ferry Bank), all requiring intensive structured support to prevent re-offending and re-enter learning.

We have identified three root causes driving this problem:
1. Absence of structured, small-group transitional bridging between open-access youth clubs and formal colleges. Young people disengaged from school lack intermediate, accredited stepping stones within walking distance.
2. Severed continuity across non-term-time windows. When term-time provision ceases, vulnerable school-leavers lose routine, leading to failed college starts in September.
3. Transport isolation and unfamiliarity with destinations beyond their immediate streets. Young people in Marlpit and Ferry Bank rarely travel outside their immediate estates without structured group transport and dedicated staff accompaniment.

Our pilot delivery proves that when structured intervention tackles these causes—combining targeted courses, off-site familiarisation trips, and intensive coordination led by Priya Raval—progression rates rise significantly, as demonstrated by the 11 young people who secured places through Second Chances in 2025.

Q3. What will you do?

Over an 18-month delivery period (April 2027 to September 2028), we will deliver the "East Kelverton Progression Pathway", expanding our tested models to support 72 young people aged 16 to 19 across Marlpit and Ferry Bank into verified education, apprenticeships, or jobs.

The project operates in three sequential phases across three 6-month cycles, engaging a cohort of 24 young people per cycle:

Phase 1: Identification and Intake (Months 1–2, 7–8, 13–14)
We will recruit 24 young people per cycle (72 total: 40 from Marlpit open-access sessions, 20 from our Ferry Bank site, and 12 referred by Barrowfield Youth Justice Team). Priya Raval will lead 1:1 intake profiling at St Aidan's Parish Hall and Ferry Bank to map educational history and individual progression goals.

Phase 2: The Second Chances Progression Course (Months 2–4, 8–10, 14–16)
Priya Raval will deliver three 12-week Second Chances courses (one per cohort, 24 participants each). Sessions will take place twice weekly at St Aidan's Parish Hall (hired at £40 per evening). The syllabus covers vocational goal-setting, functional application support, college entrance prep, and interview technique. To overcome geographic hesitation, we will run 18 off-site educational and workplace visits (6 per cohort, 18 trips total) using Northgate Minibus Hire at £95 per trip, picking up in Marlpit and Ferry Bank to take cohorts directly to Dunmore Sixth Form College and local employment hubs.

Phase 3: Summer Bridge and Transition (Months 5–6, 11–12, 17–18)
To prevent transition drop-out before autumn enrolments, we will run two intensive 9-day summer programmes (18 delivery days total across the grant) based at Wharfside Boathouse (£120 per day hire). Delivered by Priya Raval with support from trained adult volunteers from our Night Kitchen alumni, these sessions focus on enrolment verification, bridging assignments for Dunmore Sixth Form College, and workplace readiness. Redgate Catering will supply nutritious daily meals across the 18 Boathouse delivery days (£2.40 per head for 24 young people and 3 staff/volunteers, totaling £1,166.40).

Q4. What will change, and how will you know?

We will achieve four measurable progression outcomes across the 18 months:
1. 72 vulnerable young people (three cohorts of 24) will commence the Second Chances progression pathway.
2. At least 57 of the 72 starters (79 per cent; 19 per cohort) will complete the 12-week course, matching our verified pilot completion rate.
3. At least 36 young people (12 per cohort) will progress directly into a confirmed, named next step: 24 enrolling at Dunmore Sixth Form College, 6 securing formal apprenticeships, and 6 entering paid employment.
4. 100 per cent of the 12 young people referred by Barrowfield Youth Justice Team will maintain engagement without breach during their pathway participation.
