Q1. Who are you and what do you do?

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation constituted in 2016 in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of about 41,000 people in northern England. We operate on the east side of town with six paid staff (4.2 full-time equivalent) and about 25 regular volunteers. Our routine delivery comprises open-access youth sessions for 11 to 19 year olds, a weekly community supper club, and a small family advice desk.

Our core work takes place in two specific, contiguous neighbourhoods separated by the old railway infrastructure: Marlpit, the ward on the east bank of the river, and Ferry Bank, the residential estate situated north of the old goods yard. In Marlpit, we deliver open-access youth work three evenings a week. We also run our weekly supper club from St Aidan's Parish Hall on Marlpit lane, where we served 46 sittings in the year to March 2025. In October 2024, we expanded our youth delivery into Ferry Bank, establishing a second delivery base that engaged 31 young people in its first term.

Our youth provision is led by Priya Raval, our delivery coordinator since 2019, who holds a level 3 youth work qualification and personally delivers four of our weekly sessions in Marlpit. Through this presence, we have built trusted operating relationships with local services and education providers. We hold an active referral arrangement with Barrowfield Youth Justice Team, which referred nine young people to our sessions between April 2024 and March 2025, six of whom live in Ferry Bank. We also work directly with Dunmore Sixth Form College, situated one mile from our Marlpit base, to support local school-leavers into further education.

Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

The acute problem in Marlpit and Ferry Bank is the structural breakdown of transitions from secondary education and informal youth provision into sustained post-16 education, training, or employment. Young people in these two pockets face deep socio-economic isolation, compound family poverty, and a lack of bridge provision to help them cross the physical and institutional gap to mainstream post-16 destinations.

Our evidence for these conditions is direct and local:
1. Economic deprivation: At the 2021 census, 38 per cent of households in Marlpit sat in the lowest national income decile. This generational poverty restricts household capacity to underwrite transport, training materials, or protracted transitions, leaving young people vulnerable to complete disengagement at age 16.
2. Statutory disengagement and youth justice contact: Young people in Ferry Bank face particular risks around formal system involvement. Barrowfield Youth Justice Team referred nine young people to our trust in 2024-25 (six from Ferry Bank alone). Their casework confirms that unstructured time, lack of local vocational stepping stones, and disconnectedness from post-16 training perpetuate contact with statutory services.
3. Destination attrition: Simply giving young people information about opportunities does not result in enrollment. In the 2024-25 academic year, we referred 27 school-leavers from Marlpit to Dunmore Sixth Form College, yet only 18 successfully enrolled. A third dropped out before day one due to lack of transition support, travel confidence, and routine.

We ran a pilot twelve-week progression course called Second Chances in Marlpit from January to April 2025. Of the 24 young people who started, 19 completed the course, and 11 secured a college place, an apprenticeship, or paid employment. This demonstrated that structured, cohort-based transition work, backed by familiar youth workers, directly resolves destination attrition. The problem is that Ferry Bank has had no access to this model, and our Marlpit work currently lacks the capacity to run Second Chances across consecutive academic transition cycles.

Q3. What will you do?

Over an 18-month grant period from April 2027 to September 2028, Halewater Commons Trust will deliver the 'East Kelverton Progression Pathway', extending and formalising our Second Chances course across both Marlpit and Ferry Bank. We will run three sequential 12-week cohorts of Second Chances, directly supporting 72 young people (24 participants per cohort) aged 16 to 19 who are disengaged or at risk of disengagement.

The pathway runs in four distinct phases:

Phase 1: Intake, Casework, and Practical Access (Weeks 1-2 of each cohort)
Priya Raval will lead targeted intake across Marlpit and Ferry Bank. Referrals will be drawn from our open-access sessions (which engaged 31 young people in Ferry Bank in late 2024), our supper club families at St Aidan's Parish Hall, and direct formal allocations from Barrowfield Youth Justice Team (targeting 6 referrals per cohort). Each participant agrees an individual progression plan.

Phase 2: Intensive Twelve-Week Progression Delivery (Weeks 3-12)
Each cohort receives two weekly evening intensive workshops alongside their open-access attendance:
- Weekly Workshop 1 takes place at St Aidan's Parish Hall in Marlpit (hired at GBP 40 per evening session), focusing on portfolio creation, functional core skills, routine, and application management.
- Weekly Workshop 2 takes place at Wharfside Boathouse on the Ferry Bank side of the river, delivering practical vocational workshops, group resilience tasks, and employer engagement sessions.
- Transport: To ensure young people cross neighbourhood boundaries safely and access off-site vocational sites, we will contract Northgate Minibus Hire at our established rate of GBP 95 per trip, running 12 targeted minibus trips per cohort (36 trips in total across the project).

Phase 3: Formal Destination Transition and Enrolment Handover (Weeks 11-12)
Priya Raval and key youth workers will conduct joint handover sessions directly with admissions staff at Dunmore Sixth Form College (one mile from Marlpit) and local apprenticeship providers. Youth workers will accompany participants on pre-enrollment campus visits using Northgate Minibus Hire to bridge the physical transition.

Phase 4: Consolidation and Post-Progression Mentoring (Months 13-18)
During the final six months, staff will provide fortnightly peer-support check-ins for all completed cohorts at St Aidan's Parish Hall, stabilising participants in their new college, apprenticeship, and employment placements to prevent early term drop-out.

Staffing: Delivery Coordinator Priya Raval will dedicate 14 hours per week to manage the pathway, coordinate with Barrowfield Youth Justice Team, and deliver the Marlpit workshops. A sessional Youth Progression Worker will be employed for 10 hours per week across the 18 months to support Ferry Bank delivery, transport coordination, and Wharfside Boathouse sessions.

We will achieve four measurable progression outcomes across the 18-month grant:

1. Participation and Completion: 72 young people (24 per cohort across three cohorts; 36 from Marlpit, 36 from Ferry Bank, including at least 18 statutory referrals from Barrowfield Youth Justice Team) will enroll in Second Chances. At least 57 participants (79%, matching our 2025 pilot completion rate) will complete the full 12-week course.
2. Verified Progression to Defined Next Steps: A minimum of 36 young people will formally transition into a named, verified next step within three months of course completion:

We establish a definitive failure threshold: if fewer than 24 young people (out of 72 starters, or one-third) achieve a verified progression into Dunmore Sixth Form College, an apprenticeship, or paid employment by the end of the project, we will count this work as having not worked.
