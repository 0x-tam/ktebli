## Q1. Who are you and what do you do? Your organisation, the people you already work with, and the geography you work in.

Halewater Commons Trust (halewatercommons.org.uk) is a charitable incorporated organisation constituted in 2016, operating in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of about 41,000 people in northern England. We are based on the east side of town and work in the Marlpit neighbourhood.

Our team comprises six paid staff (4.2 FTE) and approximately 25 regular volunteers, with key personnel including Priya Raval. We have two years of filed accounts, with total expenditure of £214,000 in our last filed financial year, within the fund's £1.5 million ceiling.

Our core provision consists of open-access youth sessions for 11 to 19 year olds, a weekly supper club, and a small advice desk for families, delivering from St Aidan's Parish Hall. Having delivered our Second Chances programme, we work directly with local young people and families on the east side of Kelverton. This proposal addresses Round 3 Priorities 1 (work rooted in a named place: Marlpit, St Aidan's Parish Hall), 2 (progression to a defined next step), and 3 (practices that outlast the grant).

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know? Be specific about place and population. Describe the causes you intend to act on, not only the symptoms, and say what your evidence for them is.

In Marlpit, young people aged 16 to 19 face a post-GCSE transition cliff, experiencing disengagement and drop-out from further education and vocational training. While open-access youth work at St Aidan's Parish Hall provides contact for 11 to 19 year olds, leaving school creates a break in support.

The project design addresses three underlying causes of this transition failure:

- **Absence of structured pastoral casework:** Open-access youth work currently lacks a structured, one-to-one bridge to help school leavers navigate post-16 applications, admissions, and requirements within the Marlpit neighbourhood.
- **Institutional unfamiliarity and low confidence:** At-risk school leavers face acute anxiety when moving from familiar neighbourhood settings to formal, larger educational institutions.
- **Geographic and transit friction:** Physical distance between Marlpit on Kelverton's east side and regional campuses creates logistical and financial travel barriers for school leavers.

Our evidence rests on our direct delivery in Marlpit: our weekly open-access youth sessions (11-19) and family advice desk at St Aidan's Parish Hall demonstrate that local teenagers need sustained pastoral navigation to bridge the gap into post-16 destinations. Furthermore, our working relationship with Dunmore Sixth Form College identifies that structured campus familiarisation and local transit support are essential to secure enrolments from Marlpit leavers.

## Q3. What will you do? The activities, the sequence, who delivers each part, where each part happens, and how many people take part. Show how the activities follow from the causes in Q2.

We will deliver the Marlpit Post-16 Pastoral Progression Bridge over 18 months, supporting 48 NEET or at-risk young people aged 16 to 19 into verified post-16 education, apprenticeships, or accredited training. The project directly resolves the causes identified in Q2 through structured casework, transport, and college familiarisation.

### Activity Sequence

1. **Intake and Diagnostics (M1-M4):** Engage 48 participants from Marlpit via weekly youth sessions at St Aidan's Parish Hall; complete one-to-one diagnostic assessments and progression action plans.
2. **Transition Workshops (M3-M14):** Deliver small-group preparation workshops at St Aidan's focusing on institutional readiness and routine management.
3. **Supported Campus Visits (M4-M14):** Conduct 12 supported orientation visits and taster sessions to Dunmore Sixth Form College using Northgate Minibus Hire to overcome transport barriers.
4. **Casework and Enrolment (M5-M15):** Provide one-to-one navigation casework, mock interviews, and application assistance for college and training placements.
5. **Volunteer Transition Mentoring (M3-M18):** Recruit and train 12 volunteer transition mentors to provide post-enrolment check-ins and sustain retention.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know? Intended outcomes, the numbers you expect, how you will measure them, and what you would count as this work not having worked.

The project will deliver three clear outcomes:

- **Outcome 1:** 36 young people (75%) transition into a verified defined next step (further education, apprenticeship, or accredited training).
- **Outcome 2:** 30 progressed participants (83%) sustain their placement through 3-month and 6-month verification milestones.
- **Outcome 3:** A permanent, zero-cost pastoral progression protocol is embedded between Halewater Commons Trust and Dunmore Sixth Form College.

We would count this work as not having worked if fewer than 24 participants (50%) transition to a verified destination, or if fewer than 20 participants sustain their placement at the 3-month milestone.

## Q5. What happens after the grant? Name what continues, who owns it, what it costs and who pays.

- **What continues:** The joint pastoral referral protocol between open-access youth sessions and Dunmore Sixth Form College, alongside the trained volunteer mentor network.
- **Who owns it:** Jointly owned by Halewater Commons Trust's pastoral team and Dunmore Sixth Form College's admissions team.
- **What it costs:** Ongoing running costs are approximately £1,800 annually for volunteer expenses, travel, and mentor catch-up venue hire.
- **Who pays:** No future grant funding is currently secured; Halewater Commons Trust will seek local follow-on grants and core budget integration.
