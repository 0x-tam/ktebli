### Q1. Who are you and what do you do?

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation constituted in 2016. We work on the east side of Kelverton, a post-industrial town of about 41,000 people in northern England. We operate with six paid staff (4.2 full-time equivalent) and about 25 regular volunteers. Our total expenditure in the last filed financial year was £214,000, and our accounts are filed and up to date.

Our core work focuses on the east side of Kelverton, centered in the Marlpit neighbourhood. In Marlpit, we run open-access youth sessions for young people aged 11 to 19, a weekly supper club, and a small advice desk for families. We have used St Aidan's Parish Hall to bring local residents together. We also have an established relationship with Dunmore Sixth Form College, and we have previously run a programme called Second Chances. 

Through our weekly supper club and family advice desk, we work directly with households across Marlpit who face persistent low incomes, insecure work, and rising household costs. Through our youth sessions, we work with young people who have few structured opportunities outside school hours. We are a local organisation rooted in this single corner of Kelverton. Our staff and volunteers know the families in Marlpit by name, see them every week, and deliver practical support directly in the neighbourhood where they live.

### Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

The problem we intend to address is the sharp cliff-edge faced by 16-to-19-year-olds in Marlpit who disengage from education or training and have no direct, supported route back into formal study or stable employment.

We see the evidence for this weekly in Marlpit. At our open-access youth sessions and through families attending our advice desk and supper club, older teenagers frequently tell us that once they fall out of school or college, they find the formal recruitment or enrolment systems impossible to navigate on their own. They lack confidence, recent references, transport arrangements, and the routine required to re-engage with structured training. 

The immediate symptom is young people standing outside the labour market or education system with nowhere structured to go in Marlpit. The underlying causes that we can act on directly are threefold:
1. Isolation from institutional pathways. Young people who drop out of post-16 provision lose direct contact with formal pastoral support and guidance.
2. Lack of supported transition steps. Moving straight from long periods of disengagement into a rigid full-time course or job is too wide a step without transitional mentoring and confidence building.
3. Practical and geographic barriers. Getting from Marlpit to training providers across town is difficult without established travel habits or assisted transport routines.

We know these causes exist because our youth workers deal with them every week at St Aidan's Parish Hall and during our youth sessions. We have also seen this dynamic through our relationship with Dunmore Sixth Form College, where young people who drop out find it hard to re-enter without intermediate support. We intend to act directly on these three causes by restarting and expanding our Second Chances programme specifically for 16-to-19-year-olds in Marlpit.

### Q3. What will you do?

We will deliver a 15-month structured progression programme called Second Chances for 36 young people aged 16 to 19 in Marlpit who are not in education, employment, or training. We will run the programme in three sequential cohorts of 12 young people. Each cohort will take part in a 16-week cycle of guided support, skill-building, and supported transition.

The sequence for each cohort is:

Weeks 1–4: Engagement and Action Planning.
We will identify and recruit 12 young people from Marlpit through our open-access youth sessions, supper club, and family advice desk. Sessions will run twice weekly at St Aidan's Parish Hall. Staff member Priya Raval and our youth team will carry out one-to-one diagnostic assessments with each participant, setting a clear personal progression goal: enrolment at Dunmore Sixth Form College, entry into an apprenticeship, or a place in accredited vocational training.

Weeks 5–10: Core Skills and Structured Routine.
Participants will attend two structured three-hour workshops each week at St Aidan's Parish Hall. These sessions will cover workplace communication, application writing, interview practice, routine building, and personal budgeting. We will provide hot meals at each session, drawing on the kitchen setup of our weekly supper club. 

Weeks 11–14: Practical Exposure and Taster Days.
We will organise direct taster visits and introductory days at Dunmore Sixth Form College and vocational training sites in Kelverton. To overcome local transport barriers and build confidence with unfamiliar routes, we will hire minibuses from our regular supplier, Northgate Minibus Hire, to take cohorts directly to Dunmore Sixth Form College for campus tours, course taster sessions, and registration meetings.

Weeks 15–16: Formal Handover and Enrolment.
Priya Raval and our delivery staff will support each participant through the formal application and enrolment process with Dunmore Sixth Form College or other local training providers. Staff will accompany young people to their registration interviews and confirm their start dates.

Over 15 months, our staff will deliver three complete 16-week cycles, working directly with 36 young people from Marlpit.

### Q4. What will change, and how will you know?

We expect the following specific outcomes across the 15 months of delivery:
- 36 young people aged 16 to 19 from Marlpit will start the Second Chances programme.
- 30 young people (83%) will complete the full 16-week programme.
- 24 young people (67% of starters) will progress into a verified, nameable next step within one month of completing their cohort: either enrolled on a course at Dunmore Sixth Form College, registered in accredited further education, or started in a verified apprenticeship or job.
- 30 young people will complete a structured travel and institutional transition plan, demonstrating independent travel routines to Dunmore Sixth Form College and across Kelverton.

We will measure these outcomes by collecting concrete records:
1. Intake files recording employment and educational status at week one.
2. Attendance registers at St Aidan's Parish Hall for all group sessions and workshops.
3. Formal written confirmation of application, enrolment, or employment offers from Dunmore Sixth Form College and other receiving providers.
4. A follow-up check at 30 days and 90 days after completion to verify continued attendance at the destination institution.

What we would count as this work not having worked:
If fewer than 18 young people (50% of starts) achieve a verified progression to Dunmore Sixth Form College or another named education or employment destination, or if participants drop out of their progression step within 30 days of enrolment, we will treat the model as having failed to provide sufficient transitional support. If that occurs, we will conduct an immediate review with Dunmore Sixth Form College to determine whether the barriers were transport, course suitability, or a need for longer pre-enrolment preparation.

### Q5. What happens after the grant?

The Second Chances transition pathway will become a permanent part of Halewater Commons Trust's regular delivery model in Marlpit after the grant ends.

Halewater Commons Trust will own and run the ongoing activity. The permanent elements that will continue are:
1. The structured referral and transition pathway into Dunmore Sixth Form College, maintained by our core youth team during our standard open-access sessions.
2. The pre-enrolment support sessions at St Aidan's Parish Hall, integrated into our weekly youth schedule.
3. The peer mentoring network formed by the 24 progressing participants, who will be invited back to our weekly supper club to mentor newer youth club attendees.

Once the initial programme setup, curriculum materials, and college progression arrangements are established during the grant, the ongoing operational cost to maintain this progression work will be approximately £4,200 per year. This ongoing cost covers venue hire at St Aidan's Parish Hall and workshop materials. 

Halewater Commons Trust will fund this £4,200 annual cost from our core operating budget (which had an annual expenditure of £214,000 in our last filed accounts). The intense development phase funded by the Wren Hill Foundation will have established the working relationship, the transport routines, and the referral mechanisms with Dunmore Sixth Form College, allowing us to keep moving young people from Marlpit into education year after year without requiring further grant funding for this programme design.
