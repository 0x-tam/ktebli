## Q1. Who are you and what do you do? Your organisation, the people you already work with, and the geography you work in.

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation founded in 2016 in Kelverton, northern England. With six staff (4.2 FTE), 25 regular volunteers, and an annual operating expenditure of £214,000, we deliver targeted youth work and community support across two east-side neighbourhoods:

- **Marlpit Ward:** Where 38% of households sit in the lowest national income decile. We run open-access youth clubs three evenings weekly and a supper club at St Aidan's Parish Hall, catered by Redgate Catering.
- **Ferry Bank Estate:** A delivery site established in October 2024 serving young people near the former rail yard, alongside outdoor programmes at Wharfside Boathouse.

Delivery is managed by Priya Raval (Lead Delivery Coordinator, Level 3 Youth Work qualified). Governance is led by Board Chair Delroy Ferguson, who oversees annual safeguarding reviews.

In 2024–25, we referred 27 school-leavers to Dunmore Sixth Form College (one mile from Marlpit), resulting in 18 formal enrolments. Over the same period, Barrowfield Youth Justice Team referred nine young people to our provision, including six from Ferry Bank. Off-site transport is provided by Northgate Minibus Hire.

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know? Be specific about place and population. Describe the causes you intend to act on, not only the symptoms, and say what your evidence for them is.

In Marlpit and Ferry Bank, vulnerable young people aged 16–19 face severe barriers in progressing from neighbourhood provision into sustained post-16 education, training, or employment.

In early 2025, we piloted *Second Chances*, a 12-week course in Marlpit: 19 of 24 starters completed, and 11 secured college, apprenticeship, or employment placements. The pilot and operational records revealed three root causes:

1. **The Conversion Gap Between Informal Engagement and Formal Intake:** In 2024–25, nine of our 27 referrals to Dunmore Sixth Form College dropped out between application and enrolment due to complex paperwork and lack of 1:1 transition advocacy.
2. **Institutional Anxiety and Geographic Isolation:** Young people experience substantial anxiety navigating unfamiliar campuses across town without structured, accompanied visits and peer support.
3. **The Summer Transition Void:** Between June completion and September intake, young people face an eight-week loss of routine and youth worker contact, causing placement abandonment.

Our evidence base combines 2021 census data, college conversion records, youth justice logs, and 2025 pilot monitoring data.

## Q3. What will you do? The activities, the sequence, who delivers each part, where each part happens, and how many people take part. Show how the activities follow from the causes in Q2.

Over 18 months, Halewater Commons Trust will deliver the *Second Chances Progression Pathway*, supporting 80 vulnerable young people aged 16–19 from Marlpit and Ferry Bank into verified post-16 destinations.

The project runs across four phases:

| Phase and Timeline | Activities and Venues | Delivery Lead | Participants |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Setup & Intake (M1–M2)** | Establish joint intake protocol with Dunmore Sixth Form College and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team; complete outreach. | Priya Raval and Delroy Ferguson | 20 young people (Cohort 1) |
| **Phase 2: Delivery Cycle 1 (M3–M10)** | Deliver two 12-week courses at St Aidan's Hall and Ferry Bank; 12 college visits via Northgate Minibus; 12-day Summer Bridge 1 at Wharfside Boathouse with Redgate Catering. | Sessional Progression Coach and Priya Raval | 40 participants (Cohorts 1 & 2); 20 in Summer Bridge 1 |
| **Phase 3: Delivery Cycle 2 (M11–M16)** | Deliver two 12-week courses; 12 college visits; 12-day Summer Bridge 2 at Wharfside Boathouse; train 12 alumni mentors. | Sessional Coach, Priya Raval, and peer mentors | 40 participants (Cohorts 3 & 4); 20 in Summer Bridge 2; 12 alumni mentors |
| **Phase 4: Tracking & Review (M17–M18)** | 6-month placement tracking, evaluation, and embedding post-grant intake pathways. | Priya Raval and Sessional Coach | 80 participants tracked; 42 sustained destinations verified |

Activities address the root causes from Q2:
- **Intensive 1:1 Coaching (Cause 1):** Four 12-week cohorts (20 participants each) receive action planning, application assistance, and hot meals.
- **Accompanied Campus Exposure (Cause 2):** 24 supported visits to Dunmore Sixth Form College eliminate navigation anxiety.
- **Summer Bridging Intensives (Cause 3):** Two 12-day programmes at Wharfside Boathouse maintain weekly keyworker contact throughout July and August.
- **Peer Mentoring:** 12 course alumni will be trained to co-facilitate sessions.

Staffing is provided by Priya Raval (0.4 FTE) and a Sessional Youth Progression Coach (0.4 FTE), with governance from Delroy Ferguson.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know? Intended outcomes, the numbers you expect, how you will measure them, and what you would count as this work not having worked.

The project targets the following measurable outcomes:

| Metric / Outcome | Target Number | Verification and Measurement Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Enrolment** | 80 participants (4 cohorts of 20) | Signed intake registrations and diagnostic assessments |
| **Course Completion** | 64 completing (80%) | Progression portfolios and course attendance registers |
| **Campus Exposure Visits** | 24 visits completed | Minibus transport invoices and college sign-in records |
| **Summer Bridge Completion** | 40 participants | Wharfside Boathouse daily session logs |
| **Peer Mentors Trained** | 12 alumni trained | Mentor training certifications and delivery rota logs |
| **Positive Progression** | 42 verified destinations (26 college, 10 jobs/apprenticeships, 6 training) | Written college enrolment confirmations and employment/training contracts |
| **Sustained Engagement** | 34 participants retaining destination at 6 months | 6-month follow-up verification with institutions and employers |

We would count this project as not having worked if:
- Portfolio completion falls below 60% (fewer than 48 of 80 participants).
- Fewer than 30 participants achieve a verified positive destination in education, employment, or training.
- Fewer than 50% of progressed participants sustain placements at the six-month tracking milestone.
- Dunmore Sixth Form College declines to formalise the collaborative transition protocol.

## Q5. What happens after the grant? Name what continues, who owns it, what it costs and who pays.

### What Continues
The collaborative referral and transition protocol with Dunmore Sixth Form College will be embedded into annual admissions. The 12 trained alumni peer mentors will support future cohorts during workshops and summer transitions.

### Who Owns It
Halewater Commons Trust retains operational ownership, led by Priya Raval with board oversight from Delroy Ferguson. Dunmore Sixth Form College co-owns the joint referral mechanism.

### Ongoing Costs and Financing
Post-grant annual delivery costs:
- **Venue Hire:** St Aidan's Parish Hall (£40/session) and Wharfside Boathouse (£120/day), totaling £1,600 per year.
- **Group Transport:** Campus visits via Northgate Minibus Hire, totaling £760 per year.
- **Coordination & Supervision:** Sessional support and mentor management integrated into core youth team roles (0.15 FTE).

Direct recurring costs (£2,360 annually) will be absorbed by Halewater Commons Trust's core budget (£214,000 baseline) and Dunmore Sixth Form College's widening participation resources.
