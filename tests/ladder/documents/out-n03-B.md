## Q1. Who are you and what do you do?

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation, constituted in 2016 and registered in the UK (charity number and website, halewatercommons.org.uk, on the cover form). We have two years of filed accounts and expenditure of GBP 214,000 in the last filed year, well below the GBP 1.5 million income ceiling. We hold no current Neighbourhood Futures award and are not a statutory body. The Trust applies alone, as lead and accountable body. This application is submitted ahead of the deadline of 17:00 GMT on Friday 27 November 2026.

We employ six paid staff (4.2 FTE) and work with around 25 regular volunteers. We run open-access youth sessions for 11 to 19 year olds three evenings a week, a weekly supper club, and a small family advice desk.

The place is Marlpit ward, east bank of the river in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of about 41,000 people in northern England. At the 2021 census, 38 per cent of Marlpit households sat in the lowest income decile. Dunmore Sixth Form College sits one mile from our Marlpit site, and since 2023 we have referred school-leavers there. We work in Marlpit because our building, sessions, volunteers and advice desk are already there.

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

In 2024-25 the Trust referred 27 young people from Marlpit to Dunmore Sixth Form College. Eighteen enrolled; nine did not. We do not currently track what happens to those who enrol after enrolment; building that record is part of this project.

The problem in one line: school-leavers aged 16 to 19 in Marlpit are referred onward but drop out in the gap between a school leaving date and a college start date, and again in the first weeks after enrolment. Our own referral record is the evidence of a route that works to a point and then lets go.

We act on four causes rather than the symptom of low enrolment.

1. No named person owns a case between referral and enrolment, so the summer gap goes unmanaged.
2. There is no written handover to the college, so knowledge of support needs does not travel with the young person.
3. The first six weeks after enrolment are unsupported, so early wobbles about travel, money, attendance and confidence can become withdrawals.
4. Referral is informal and event-based rather than a sequenced, recorded route with a defined next step.

The group course model works here. In 2025 the Trust ran a twelve-week progression course, Second Chances, in Marlpit from January to April: of 24 starters, 19 finished and 11 moved into a college place, apprenticeship or paid work. The design we now propose adds accompaniment after handover, a feature the Trust has not previously run. This application buys that missing accompaniment, not a new service.

## Q3. What will you do?

**Held Through: Marlpit Progression Keyworking**, 18 months, delivered in Marlpit ward and at Dunmore Sixth Form College. Target group: school-leavers aged 16 to 19, plus Year 11 leavers approaching a leaving date, identified through our existing youth sessions and family advice desk. No eligibility test beyond age, ward, and being at or past a leaving date without a held next step. Caseload: 70 young people.

The signature mechanic is a named progression keyworker who holds each young person from the youth session, through referral, and through the first six weeks after enrolment, using a written handover sheet agreed with the college.

| Months | Activity | Delivered by | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Recruit and induct one progression keyworker, 0.8 FTE | Trust manager | Marlpit premises |
| 2-18 | Rolling caseload: identification at weekly youth sessions, one-to-one plan naming the next step, contact through the summer gap | Keyworker | Marlpit and community |
| 2-4 | Agree handover protocol, building on the referral route in place since 2023 | Manager and keyworker with college | Dunmore Sixth Form College |
| 4-18 | Handover sheet completed and signed by the young person, passed to a named college contact, reviewed at enrolment | Keyworker | Both sites |
| 5-8 and 11-14 | Two twelve-week group progression courses, up to 20 each | Trust youth staff, keyworker, volunteers | Marlpit premises |
| 6-18 | Six-week accompaniment after enrolment: week 1 contact, recorded checks at weeks 2 and 6, problem-solving | Keyworker | College and Marlpit |
| 9-17 | Write up curriculum and protocol as a retained document; quarterly trustee review | Manager and trustees | Trust |
| 3-18 | Track the referral-to-enrolment-to-retention chain; independent read at month 17 | Keyworker, external reader | Trust |
| 16-18 | Embed protocol in youth session routine; hand caseload function to existing staff | Trust manager | Marlpit premises |

Each activity answers a cause: keyworker (one), handover sheet (two), six-week accompaniment (three), recorded plan and protocol (four). Dunmore is the named next-step provider. We will also approach local schools on early identification of Year 11 leavers, and employers and apprenticeship providers so an apprenticeship or paid work counts as a defined next step.

Main risks: the college declines or delays formal handover, in which case the protocol is scoped light and consent-based and worked from the Trust's side; recruitment slips, so recruitment starts at month 1 and cohort 1 not until month 5; small numbers make percentages volatile, so we report absolute numbers too.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know?

Three outcomes: more of those referred actually enrol; more of those who enrol are still in place at six weeks; and the Trust ends the grant holding a documented progression route it can run itself.

| Point in the chain | Target over 18 months |
|---|---|
| Young people on the keyworker caseload | 70 |
| Course participants | Two cohorts of up to 20 |
| Referrals taken to a defined next step | 55 of 70 |
| Enrolments or equivalent next steps | 40 of 55 |
| Still in place at six weeks | 33 of 40 |

We measure the chain per young person in a simple spreadsheet, not attendance. Baseline: our 2024-25 record of 27 referrals and 18 enrolments. We will ask the college to share retention information with consent and to name a contact for the accompaniment period. An independent read of the retention record takes place at month 17.

Failure is defined in advance: enrolment gains that do not survive six weeks, and fewer than 33 young people in place at six weeks. If the chain breaks where it breaks now, the work has not worked, and we will report that.

## Q5. What happens after the grant?

Three things continue: the handover sheet and referral protocol as a standing agreement with Dunmore; the written-up twelve-week curriculum; and the retention tracking record. All are owned by the Trust and reviewed quarterly by trustees. From month 16 the caseload function is absorbed into the existing youth sessions and held by existing staff.

Ongoing cost is low but not nil: staff time within the existing team plus modest materials and participant travel, in the low thousands a year, delivered from the Marlpit premises we already use for youth sessions. We will meet this from unrestricted income and small local grants. No future funder is secured and the design assumes none. The 0.8 FTE keyworker post does not continue unless separately funded, which is why the transferable asset is the protocol, curriculum and tracking record.
