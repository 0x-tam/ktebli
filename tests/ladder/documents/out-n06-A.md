## Q1. Who are you and what do you do? Your organisation, the people you already work with, and the geography you work in.

Halewater Commons Trust is a registered charitable incorporated organisation constituted in 2016 (website: halewatercommons.org.uk). We operate in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of approximately 41,000 residents in northern England, with our work rooted in Marlpit ward on the east bank of the river. In our last filed financial year, total expenditure was GBP 214,000, and we have two full years of filed accounts, confirming an income under GBP 1.5 million.

We run three weekly open-access youth sessions for 11 to 19-year-olds in Marlpit, a weekly supper club at St Aidan's Parish Hall, and a family advice desk. Our operational team comprises six paid staff (4.2 full-time equivalent) and approximately 25 regular volunteers. Youth delivery is led by Priya Raval, Delivery Coordinator since 2019, who holds a Level 3 youth work qualification and runs four of our weekly sessions in Marlpit.

This proposal addresses all three Round 3 priorities: rooted delivery in a named place (Marlpit ward), progression to defined destinations (college, apprenticeships, and employment), and sustainable practices outlasting the grant. We refer school-leavers to Dunmore Sixth Form College, situated one mile from our base (referring 27 and enrolling 18 in 2024-25). ## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know? Be specific about place and population. Describe the causes you intend to act on, not only the symptoms, and say what your evidence for them is.

In Marlpit ward, 38 per cent of households sit in the lowest income decile (2021 census). Young people aged 16 to 19 in Marlpit face substantial barriers entering education, training, or sustained employment. While NEET status is the visible symptom, our front-line work identifies three actionable causes:

1. **Low educational confidence and pathway disorientation:** Disrupted educational histories leave young people with low academic self-efficacy, limited knowledge of vocational routes, and unaddressed qualification gaps.
2. **Institutional and travel friction:** Although Dunmore Sixth Form College is one mile away, young people experience severe campus hesitation, travel anxiety, and unfamiliarity with post-16 environments.
3. **Absence of structured 1-to-1 navigation:** Mainstream services rarely provide the intensive individual support required to assemble documentation, complete applications, and navigate admissions.

Our evidence comes from our 12-week Second Chances pilot in Marlpit (January to April 2025), where 19 of 24 starters completed and 11 secured college places, apprenticeships, or paid work, alongside continuous referral logs with Dunmore Sixth Form College.

## Q3. What will you do? The activities, the sequence, who delivers each part, where each part happens, and how many people take part. Show how the activities follow from the causes in Q2.

Over 18 months, Halewater Commons Trust will deliver *Second Chances: Marlpit Youth Progression Programme*, engaging 72 participants aged 16 to 19 across three cohorts of 24. Total grant funding requested is GBP 43,456. Delivery will take place at St Aidan's Parish Hall and Dunmore Sixth Form College.

### Structured Activities

1. **Intake and Assessment (Months 1-14):** Priya Raval (0.4 FTE) and the Sessional Youth Support Worker (0.2 FTE) profile 72 participants at weekly Marlpit sessions to identify personal barriers and vocational goals.
2. **Second Chances Progression Courses (Months 3-15):** Three 12-week cohorts (36 two-hour sessions) at St Aidan's Parish Hall (GBP 40/session; GBP 1,440 total) building communication, vocational skills, and application readiness. Target: 57 completers.
3. **Supported Campus Transition Days (Months 3-15):** Eighteen minibus trips (six per cohort) via Northgate Minibus Hire (GBP 95/trip; GBP 1,710 total) to Dunmore Sixth Form College for tutor introductions and taster workshops.
4. **1-to-1 Mentoring and Application Clinics (Months 3-17):** Delivery team and trained guides provide 450 hours of individual application and interview mentoring at St Aidan's Parish Hall.
5. **Volunteer Progression Guide Rota (Months 2-16):** Twelve volunteers complete structured training and DBS checks to support ongoing cohort navigation.
6. **Destination and Retention Audits (Months 6-18):** The Monitoring and Administration Lead (0.1 FTE) tracks positive destinations and conducts 6-month retention audits.

## Q5. What happens after the grant? Name what continues, who owns it, what it costs and who pays.

Following the 18-month grant period, three assets will continue in Marlpit ward under the ownership of Halewater Commons Trust, managed by Priya Raval and the youth delivery team:

1. **Second Chances Curriculum:** The 12-week progression modules and diagnostic tools will remain permanent trust assets integrated into regular Marlpit youth sessions.
2. **Volunteer Progression Rota:** The 12 trained Volunteer Progression Guides will provide ongoing application and mentoring touchpoints within existing weekly sessions.
3. **College Referral Protocol:** The institutional referral and campus transition protocol with Dunmore Sixth Form College will continue at zero marginal cost.

Ongoing volunteer-led progression touchpoints will be integrated directly into our core open-access Marlpit youth session budgets while continuation funding is sought, requiring no dedicated continuation grant to sustain basic operations.
