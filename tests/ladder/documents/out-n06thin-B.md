## Q1. Who are you and what do you do?

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation, constituted in 2016, based in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of about 41,000 people in northern England. Our registration is current and in good standing (halewatercommons.org.uk). We have six paid staff (4.2 FTE), around 25 regular volunteers, expenditure of GBP 214,000 in the last filed year, and two years of accounts filed.

We run open-access youth sessions for 11 to 19 year olds, a weekly supper club and a small family advice desk on the east side of town, including **Marlpit**, the neighbourhood for this application. This is not entry into a new place: it adds a progression function to youth work we already deliver.

We also have an existing relationship with **Dunmore Sixth Form College**, have previously used **St Aidan's Parish Hall** as a venue and **Northgate Minibus Hire** for transport, and have run a programme called Second Chances. This application puts those arrangements on a formal, project-specific footing: a signed progression agreement, booked hall time for handovers, and hire agreed per visit. Priya Raval is among those involved in the Trust. We apply as a single organisation and are the lead and accountable body.

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

For 16 to 19 year olds in Marlpit who have disengaged from school or college, the transition point itself is unsupported. Open-access provision is designed and funded for attendance, not progression casework: a service can be well attended while no named person carries responsibility for an individual as they leave it. Where nothing is written down, dated and owned, an intention cannot be followed up.

Honestly, we do not hold destination data for our 16 to 19 year olds. We are not claiming a measured local failure rate; the function that would produce that evidence does not exist in our service, and this application creates it. Destination baselines are set in the first eight weeks of casework and reported as found, whether or not they flatter us.

The design removes five features of the transition:

1. No caseload: no single worker holds an individual through the point of leaving.
2. No warm handover: a young person is pointed at provision with nobody accompanying them to the door.
3. No named contact on the receiving side: a stalled application has nobody to chase it.
4. No familiarity: provider sites need a journey not made before, at the young person's cost.
5. No written plan: without a dated, co-signed document there is nothing to check against.

This grant buys the last hundred metres to a named door, where our contact currently ends.

## Q3. What will you do?

**The Last Hundred Metres: Marlpit Progression Keyworking**, 18 months, working with 60 young people aged 16 to 19 recruited through keyworker conversation at our existing sessions, supper club and advice desk rather than by open advertising.

| Months | Activity | Who delivers | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Recruit and induct one progression keyworker, 0.8 FTE, line-managed in the existing team | Senior staff | Trust |
| 1-4 | Sign a bilateral progression agreement with Dunmore Sixth Form College naming contacts each side, entry routes, enrolment dates, termly meeting; approach two further providers on the same terms | Senior staff and keyworker | College and Trust |
| 2-17 | Keyworker attends sessions and supper club, opens case records, holds fortnightly one-to-ones | Keyworker | Sessions and booked hall time |
| 4-16 | Small-group escorted visits to named provision with debrief; transport hired per visit | Keyworker and volunteers | Provider sites |
| 4-17 | Dated next-step plan per young person; warm handover meeting with receiving provider; both parties co-sign | Keyworker and provider contact | St Aidan's Parish Hall |
| 7-18 | Three-month retention check, with re-plan offer where the step has not held | Keyworker | Sessions and by phone |
| 6-15 | Draft progression pathway and handover protocol from live casework, revised twice | Keyworker and senior staff | Trust |
| 14-18 | Train six paid staff and interested volunteers; embed a progression check in session routine | Senior staff | Booked venue |

Phases: set up (months 1-4), core delivery (4-14), retention checks, protocol and embedding (14-18). Each activity answers one of the five features above.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know?

The defined next step is enrolment at Dunmore Sixth Form College, an apprenticeship or training place, employment, or a named local support service. The receiving provider takes them on; we do not deliver the next step.

| Indicator | Target |
|---|---|
| Case records opened, aged 16-19 | 58 of a planned 60 |
| One-to-one keyworking sessions | 430 |
| Co-signed, dated next-step plans | 48 |
| Transport-assisted journeys | 210 |
| Taking up a named next step within three months of sign-off | 31 of 48 |
| Still in that step, or an agreed alternative, at six months | 22 of 31 |
| Confidence increase on a 5-item intake and exit scale | 34 matched pairs |
| Staff and volunteers trained in the protocol | 14 |
| Protocol adopted as standing practice | By month 17 |

Output baselines are zero, because neither the post nor the plan format exists. We count only arrivals verified by the named receiving provider for young people on the recorded caseload, plus the retention check, and make no wider claims.

Failure is defined openly as plans written but not taken up: if 48 plans are signed and fewer than 31 young people walk through a named door, that is reported as a shortfall with reasons. Risks: college capacity (mitigated by two further providers), recruitment delay (targets set for a 14-month delivery window inside 18 months), and over-reliance on one worker (mitigated by protocol drafting from month 6 and training in months 14-18).

## Q5. What happens after the grant?

What continues is the written progression pathway and handover protocol, the practice of every 16 to 19 year old leaving with a written, dated, co-signed next-step plan, trained staff and volunteers, and a minuted termly progression meeting with Dunmore Sixth Form College.

Halewater Commons Trust owns all of it. The ongoing cost is a fraction of existing staff time, roughly half a day per fortnight across the team, plus occasional minibus hire in the low hundreds of pounds a year, absorbed into already-staffed sessions as a change of practice rather than an added service.

We make no claim that the 0.8 FTE post continues. We have no future funding evidenced for it, and the design deliberately does not depend on it.
