Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

The problem is the lack of structured local progression routes into education, training, and employment for young people aged 16 to 19 living in the Marlpit ward of Kelverton. 

At the 2021 census, 38 per cent of households in Marlpit were in the lowest income decile. This makes Marlpit the most economically disadvantaged ward on the east bank of the river in Kelverton. The families who use our weekly supper club and advice desk face persistent low incomes, which narrows the practical choices open to school-leavers and young adults.

From our weekly open-access youth sessions, we see that many 16 to 19 year olds in Marlpit leave school without securing a college place, an apprenticeship, or a job. They spend long periods out of structured education and training. Without targeted support, they do not make the transition across the river or up to local post-16 institutions. 

Our evidence from direct delivery shows both the barrier and what resolves it:
1. In the 2024–25 academic year, when we provided structured referrals and follow-up support, 18 out of 27 young people from Marlpit (66.7 per cent) successfully enrolled at Dunmore Sixth Form College, located one mile away. Without this referral link, school-leavers who attend open-access sessions do not navigate admissions independently.
2. During our pilot of the twelve-week Second Chances course in Marlpit (January to April 2025), 24 young people enrolled. Five dropped out, but 19 completed the twelve weeks (79.2 per cent completion), and 11 secured an immediate positive destination in college, an apprenticeship, or paid employment (45.8 per cent of starters). 

The underlying cause of low post-16 participation in Marlpit is not a lack of local provision, but the absence of sustained, trusted, step-by-step guidance that takes a young person from an informal community setting into a formal education or workplace setting. Dunmore Sixth Form College is one mile from Marlpit, yet many young people do not cross that distance without direct support, preparation, and transport.
