## Q1. Who are you and what do you do? Your organisation, the people you already work with, and the geography you work in.

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation constituted in 2016 in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of approximately 41,000 residents in northern England. The Trust operates with six paid staff (4.2 full-time equivalent) and 25 regular volunteers, with total expenditure in the last filed financial year of £214,000 across two years of filed accounts.

Our operational focus is Marlpit, an urban ward on the east bank of the river where 38 per cent of households sat in the lowest national income decile at the 2021 census. On the east side of town, we deliver:
- Open-access youth sessions running three evenings a week for young people aged 11 to 19.
- A weekly community supper club.
- A walk-in advice desk for local families.

These services provide continuous contact with local young people and families facing severe economic disadvantage.

We have established direct progression links for local school-leavers. In the 2024–25 academic year, the Trust referred 27 young people to Dunmore Sixth Form College, located one mile from our Marlpit site, resulting in 18 enrolments. From January to April 2025, we ran "Second Chances", a 12-week progression pilot in Marlpit: 24 young people started, 19 completed, and 11 progressed into a college place, an apprenticeship, or paid employment. This proposal expands that model into an 18-month structured pathway directly addressing all three Round 3 priorities: place-based delivery in Marlpit, verified progression to named destinations, and sustainable practices that outlast the grant.

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know? Be specific about place and population. Describe the causes you intend to act on, not only the symptoms, and say what your evidence for them is.

In Marlpit, disengaged 16- to 19-year-olds face acute barriers moving from informal community settings into structured education, training, or sustained employment.

While youth disengagement and low local progression rates are visible symptoms, our frontline delivery identifies three specific operational causes:

1. **Progression vacuum in informal youth spaces:** Young people attend regular evening youth clubs and community sessions but lack structured, low-barrier navigation within those trusted spaces to turn informal contact into concrete career and educational pathways.
2. **Institutional and procedural friction:** Mainstream application processes, travel logistics across town, and unfamiliar formal environments present major hurdles that cause young people to drop out during enrolment transitions.
3. **Absence of early post-transition retention support:** Once enrolled or placed, young people lack structured check-ins during the first 90 days to resolve initial transport, financial, or workplace challenges before they result in disengagement.

Our evidence rests on local intake and delivery data: 38% of Marlpit households sit in the lowest income decile; our 2024–25 referral baseline with Dunmore Sixth Form College showed that 18 of 27 referred young people successfully enrolled; and our January–April 2025 Second Chances pilot demonstrated that 19 of 24 starters completed the course and 11 secured an immediate college place, apprenticeship, or job.

## Q3. What will you do? The activities, the sequence, who delivers each part, where each part happens, and how many people take part. Show how the activities follow from the causes in Q2.

The Trust will deliver "Second Chances: Marlpit Youth Progression Pathway", supporting 66 disengaged young people aged 16 to 19 across three sequential 12-week cohorts over 18 months (22 participants per cohort).

### How Activities Address Root Causes

- **Overcoming the progression vacuum (Cause 1):** Delivery staff engage participants directly during open-access evening sessions and the weekly supper club, using established relationships to onboard them into structured 12-week cohorts.
- **Removing procedural friction (Cause 2):** Tailored 1-to-1 action planning provides direct help with application forms, travel costs, and interview preparation, accompanied by supported visits to Dunmore Sixth Form College.
- **Securing sustained retention (Cause 3):** Dedicated contact at 30, 60, and 90 days post-placement resolves initial operational or personal hurdles to sustain engagement.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know? Intended outcomes, the numbers you expect, how you will measure them, and what you would count as this work not having worked.

The project will achieve four defined outcomes across 18 months:

1. **Curriculum Completion:** 52 participants (79% of starters) complete the 12-week curriculum with a verified individual progression plan.
2. **Positive Destination Progression:** 34 participants (52% of starters) progress into a verified destination (Dunmore Sixth Form College, an apprenticeship, or paid employment).
3. **Sustained Placement:** 28 participants (82% of placed individuals) sustain their placement for at least 90 days.
4. **Permanent Systemic Integration:** A permanent progression intake and referral toolkit is fully embedded into Halewater Commons Trust's ongoing youth sessions.

### Measurement Framework

| Metric | Target | Verification Method & Source | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Second Chances Enrolment | 66 young people | Intake logs and baseline registers | Start of each cohort |
| Completed Progression Plan | 52 young people | Signed individual progression portfolios | Week 12 of each cohort |
| Confirmed Positive Destination | 34 young people | Enrolment confirmation, apprenticeship agreements, employer letters | Monthly post-cohort |
| Sustained 90-Day Placement | 28 young people | 90-day review records and provider check-ins | 30, 60, 90 days post-placement |

### Project Non-Performance Criteria

We would count this work as not having worked if fewer than 40 participants complete individual action plans, if fewer than 25 secure a verified positive progression destination, or if 90-day retention falls below 60 per cent of placed individuals.

## Q5. What happens after the grant? Name what continues, who owns it, what it costs and who pays.

The 12-week Second Chances curriculum, the 1-to-1 intake toolkit, and the structured referral corridor to Dunmore Sixth Form College will continue as permanent operating practices embedded within our weekly Marlpit youth provision.

### Who Owns It
Halewater Commons Trust's core youth work team (4.2 FTE) owns the delivery model, intake tools, and referral relationships.

- **Staffing and Mentoring:** Delivered by our permanent core youth work team as part of regular open-access sessions.
