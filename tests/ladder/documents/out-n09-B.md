## Q1. Who are you and what do you do?

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation, constituted in 2016 and registered in the UK, based in Kelverton, a post-industrial town of about 41,000 people in northern England. Registration is confirmed and accounts are filed for the last two years; expenditure in the last filed year was GBP 214,000. We have six paid staff (4.2 FTE) and about 25 regular volunteers (halewatercommons.org.uk). We apply as a single organisation and are the lead, accountable body.

We run open-access youth sessions for 11 to 19 year olds, a weekly supper club and a family advice desk on the east side of town. Our open-access youth work operates in Marlpit ward, on the east bank of the river, where 38 per cent of households sat in the lowest income decile at the 2021 census. The supper club uses St Aidan's Parish Hall in Marlpit, and served 46 sittings in the year to March 2025. A second delivery site opened in Ferry Bank, the estate north of the old goods yard, in October 2024, attended by 31 young people in its first term. Youth work is led by Priya Raval, delivery coordinator since 2019, who holds a level 3 youth work qualification and personally runs four of the six weekly Marlpit sessions.

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know?

From January to April 2025 we ran a twelve-week progression course, Second Chances. Twenty-four young people started, 19 finished, and 11 moved into a college place, an apprenticeship or paid work. So of those who started, fewer than half reached a next step.

Our referral figures show a similar shape. In the 2024-25 academic year we referred 27 school-leavers to Dunmore Sixth Form College and 18 enrolled. Referral to a named provider already happens; the recorded evidence stops at enrolment, and we hold no data on what happens to a young person after that point.

Referral routes also bring high-need young people: Barrowfield Youth Justice Team sent nine young people to us between April 2024 and March 2025, six of them living in Ferry Bank.

Our working hypothesis, which the funnel counts in Q4 are designed to test rather than to assume, is that the losses sit in the steps between finishing a course and settling into a place: application, interview, enrolment paperwork and the first weeks of attendance. The project therefore attaches a named person to each of those steps and records what happens at each one. If the design is wrong, the funnel will show it.

Marlpit is the right place: our youth work operates there, St Aidan's Parish Hall is there, Dunmore Sixth Form College is the named next-step provider for school-leavers we already refer, and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team refers into the same catchment.

## Q3. What will you do?

Marlpit Next Step runs for 18 months in Marlpit ward, funding the missing part: walking through the college door with a young person and staying until they are settled.

1. **Months 1-2, set up.** Recruit a 0.6 FTE progression keyworker and induct them alongside Priya Raval. Rebuild Second Chances as a 12-week course with explicit application, interview and enrolment weeks, written up as a reusable document.
2. **Months 2-5.** Agree a written referral and handover agreement with Dunmore Sixth Form College covering timing, contact points and out-of-window places.
3. **Months 3-6, 7-10 and 11-14.** Deliver three cohorts of 22 (66 in total) at St Aidan's Parish Hall and the existing Marlpit youth site, led by Priya Raval (0.2 FTE course lead and supervision) with existing sessional workers. Recruitment comes from our Marlpit youth work, Barrowfield referrals and young people already heading to Dunmore. Cohorts are timed to enrolment windows.
4. **Months 6, 10 and 14.** A completion-to-enrolment handover meeting for every finisher, with the young person, the keyworker and the named provider, recorded in writing.
5. **Months 6-16.** The keyworker "stay-with-them" phase: paperwork, interview, enrolment and weekly contact through the first six weeks of attendance, with a recorded six-week check and re-engagement where attendance slips. It begins during the course, so completion is not a cliff edge.
6. **Months 15-18.** Fold the curriculum, handover protocol and six-week check into the standing Marlpit youth sessions and brief staff and volunteers.

Transport for college and employer visits is bought from Northgate Minibus Hire at GBP 95 a trip, the rate paid across 14 trips between April 2024 and March 2025. Where a next step is work rather than college, employers and apprenticeship providers receive the application and join the handover meeting. If recruitment slips, Priya Raval covers keyworker duties for cohort one and the timetable shifts rather than compresses.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know?

| Indicator | Baseline | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Starting a cohort | 24 started in 2025 | 66 |
| Completing a cohort | 19 of 24 | 52 |
| Handover meetings held | New indicator | 52 |
| Place offer from named next step | New indicator | 44 |
| Enrolled at named next step | 18 of 27 referred; 11 of 24 starters | 36 |
| Still attending at six weeks | New indicator | 29 |
| Strand adopted into standing sessions | New indicator | Board-approved by month 18 |

Method: weekly registers, a signed handover record per young person, offer letters and enrolment confirmation from the provider, and a keyworker six-week check with the young person and their tutor. We review the funnel after each cohort and adjust the next. A light-touch external evaluation at month 17 tests the figures. If the proportion enrolling does not exceed our 2024-25 referral pattern, the design has not worked and we will say so.

## Q5. What happens after the grant?

Four things continue: the 12-week curriculum, the handover protocol, the six-week check, and the written agreement with Dunmore Sixth Form College. We own all four outright, and by month 18 they run as a progression strand inside the existing Marlpit youth sessions rather than as a separate course.

Continuing cost is marginal: hire of St Aidan's Parish Hall for group sessions, occasional minibus trips, and coordinator and sessional time reallocated within the sessions, paid from our existing Marlpit delivery budget. The 0.6 FTE keyworker post is grant-funded and does not continue unless further funding is raised; none is secured and we claim none. The durable value sits in documented protocol and staff practice, not in the post.
