## Q1. Who are you and what do you do? Your organisation, the people you already work with, and the geography you work in.

Halewater Commons Trust is a charitable incorporated organisation established in 2016 in Kelverton, an industrial town of 41,000 residents in northern England. In our last filed financial year, organisational expenditure was GBP 214,000 across two complete years of filed accounts. We employ six staff (4.2 full-time equivalent) supported by 25 regular volunteers.

We deliver neighbourhood youth work for young people aged 11 to 19, a weekly supper club, and family advice across two east Kelverton neighbourhoods:

- **Marlpit:** Open-access youth sessions run three evenings weekly. Our supper club hosted 46 sittings at St Aidan's Parish Hall in the year to March 2025.
- **Ferry Bank:** A dedicated site opened in October 2024 on the Ferry Bank estate, engaging 31 young people in its opening term.

Delivery Coordinator Priya Raval (Level 3 Youth Work, in post since 2019) leads youth delivery and manages four weekly sessions in Marlpit. We support vulnerable school-leavers across both localities. Since 2023, we have linked school-leavers with Dunmore Sixth Form College, situated one mile from Marlpit; in 2024-25, 27 young people were referred and 18 enrolled. Between April 2024 and March 2025, Barrowfield Youth Justice Team referred nine young people to our provision, including six residing in Ferry Bank.

This project delivers:
1. *Place-based delivery:* Rooted at St Aidan's Parish Hall and Wharfside Boathouse alongside Dunmore Sixth Form College and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team.
2. *Defined progression:* Securing transitions into college courses, apprenticeships, and employment.
3. *Lasting legacy:* Embedding a multi-agency progression pathway and peer mentoring within our core model.

## Q2. What is the problem, and how do you know? Be specific about place and population. Describe the causes you intend to act on, not only the symptoms, and say what your evidence for them is.

Young people aged 16 to 19 in Marlpit and Ferry Bank face severe barriers moving from open community sessions into sustained education, training, or employment. While drop-in sessions provide safe local engagement, young people lacking structured transition support regularly fail to convert referrals into completed college enrolments or lasting jobs.

The target group comprises 16-to-19-year-olds in Marlpit and Ferry Bank who are NEET or at imminent risk of becoming NEET. In Marlpit, 38 per cent of households fall within the lowest national income decile (Census 2021). At Ferry Bank, delivery confirmed high demand, engaging 31 young people in autumn 2024 alongside six statutory referrals from Barrowfield Youth Justice Team.

We act on three underlying causes:

1. **Absence of modular preparation:** Drop-in sessions offer engagement but lack the structured, stepped curriculum needed to build routine, confidence, and vocational readiness prior to college or work.
2. **Unaccompanied institutional navigation:** Disadvantaged young people struggle to complete admissions, campus visits, and cross-town travel independently without dedicated keyworker accompaniment.
3. **Unstructured referral handoffs:** Interfaces between youth provision, youth justice, and post-16 providers operate informally, leaving participants unsupported between initial referral and active attendance.

Evidence base:
- *Census 2021:* 38 per cent of Marlpit households sit in the lowest national income decile.
- *Internal records (2024-25):* Of 27 school-leavers referred to Dunmore Sixth Form College, 18 enrolled while nine dropped out before completing enrolment.
- *Pilot outcomes:* Our Second Chances 12-week pilot (January to April 2025, Marlpit) proved that structured modular support enabled 19 of 24 starters to complete, with 11 securing college, apprenticeship, or job placements.
- *Local intake:* 31 young people engaged at Ferry Bank in autumn 2024, and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team referred nine young people in 2024-25.

## Q3. What will you do? The activities, the sequence, who delivers each part, where each part happens, and how many people take part. Show how the activities follow from the causes in Q2.

Halewater Commons Trust will deliver *Second Chances: Marlpit & Ferry Bank Progression Pathway* over 18 months, engaging 76 young people aged 16 to 19 across four 12-week cohorts. The grant requested is GBP 53,738 over 18 months.

| Activity & Sequence | Schedule | Delivery Lead | Location & Venue | Target Scale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Outreach & diagnostic action planning** | Months 1-18 (4 cohorts) | Priya Raval (0.4 FTE) & Youth Support Worker (0.5 FTE) | Marlpit sessions & Ferry Bank site | 76 young people enrolled & action planned |
| **2. Stepped modular groupwork** | Months 2-16 (Cohort 1: M2-4; C2: M6-8; C3: M10-12; C4: M14-16) | Priya Raval & Youth Support Worker | St Aidan's Parish Hall & Ferry Bank | 48 weekly sessions; 60 completers |
| **3. Supported college taster visits** | Months 3-16 (4 visits per cohort) | Priya Raval & Youth Support Worker | Dunmore Sixth Form College (Northgate Minibus Hire) | 16 minibus trips; 60 participants |
| **4. Keyworking & destination tracking** | Months 2-18 | Youth Support Worker (supervised by P. Raval) | Delivery sites & destination providers | 76 supported; 36 placed; 28 tracked at 3 & 6 mos |
| **5. Summer progression intensives** | Months 7-9 (Yr 1) & Months 15-17 (Yr 2) | Priya Raval & Youth Support Worker | Wharfside Boathouse (Ferry Bank side) | 12 full days (6 per year); 40 participants |
| **6. Peer mentor training** | Months 4-18 | Priya Raval | St Aidan's Parish Hall | 10 alumni trained as volunteer peer mentors |

Addressing the root causes:
- *Cause 1 (Lack of modular preparation):* 48 stepped groupwork sessions and 12 summer progression days build vocational skills and structured routines.
- *Cause 2 (Navigation and travel barriers):* 16 supported college visits via Northgate Minibus Hire and dedicated keyworker accompaniment remove travel anxiety and unfamiliarity.
- *Cause 3 (Unstructured handoffs):* Joint referral protocols with Barrowfield Youth Justice Team and Dunmore Sixth Form College ensure coordinated progression, reinforced by 10 peer mentors.

Staffing: Delivery Coordinator Priya Raval (0.4 FTE, 18 months) oversees curriculum quality and partner coordination. A Youth Support Worker (0.5 FTE, 18 months) provides direct keyworking, group delivery, and destination tracking.

## Q4. What will change, and how will you know? Intended outcomes, the numbers you expect, how you will measure them, and what you would count as this work not having worked.

The project will deliver three core outcomes over 18 months:
- **Outcome 1:** 36 young people (60 per cent of 60 completers) transition into a verified positive destination (further education, apprenticeship, accredited training, or paid employment).
- **Outcome 2:** 28 young people sustain their destination placement at 3-month and 6-month tracking checkpoints.
- **Outcome 3:** A unified multi-agency progression pathway is embedded across Halewater Commons Trust, Dunmore Sixth Form College, and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team.

### What We Would Count as Not Having Worked

We would count the project as not having worked if:
1. Fewer than 48 participants (under 63 per cent of starters) complete the 12-week pathway;
2. Fewer than 25 completers (under 42 per cent) achieve a verified education, training, or employment destination;
3. Fewer than 18 placed participants sustain their destination at the 3-month checkpoint;
4. Partner agencies fail to maintain joint protocols and return to fragmented referrals.

If quarterly milestone reviews show performance falling below 80 per cent of targets, the Delivery Coordinator will restructure session times, increase direct outreach, and add supported campus visits.

## Q5. What happens after the grant? Name what continues, who owns it, what it costs and who pays.

### What Continues
1. **The Progression Pathway:** Joint referral and transition protocols connecting Marlpit and Ferry Bank to Dunmore Sixth Form College and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team remain operational.
2. **Peer Mentoring Scheme:** The 10 trained alumni mentors will continue supporting incoming cohorts in regular sessions.
3. **Curriculum Toolkits:** The 12-week modular progression curriculum and transition toolkits will remain in active use.

### Who Owns It
Halewater Commons Trust retains operational ownership of the progression curriculum and volunteer mentor management, led by Delivery Coordinator Priya Raval. Dunmore Sixth Form College owns college admissions liaison, and Barrowfield Youth Justice Team owns youth justice referral pathways.

### What It Costs
Following the grant period, development costs (dedicated project keyworking, boathouse summer hire, and minibus charters) conclude. Ongoing marginal costs to maintain the modular pathway within regular delivery comprise:
- St Aidan's Parish Hall hire: GBP 40 per youth session.
- Volunteer mentor resources and materials: GBP 60 per month.
- Total marginal cost: approximately GBP 2,600 annually.

### Who Pays
Halewater Commons Trust will absorb these marginal costs directly into its annual operating budget (expenditure of GBP 214,000 in the last filed year) as part of core youth delivery, requiring no further foundation funding.
