# The referent ladder — result

**Date:** 2026-08-27 · **Status:** PARTIALLY MEASURED. 4 of 10 critic cells obtained.
**Headline:** the ladder is flat where it was expected to rise, and null where it was expected
to separate. On the evidence in hand, **evidence volume is not the binding constraint on
fundability, and the intake-expansion decision of 2026-08-26 is not supported by this run.**

That is not the answer anyone wanted, including me. Section 0 says exactly how weak the
evidence for it is, and section 9 says what would overturn it.

---

## 0. Limits, first

Read these before any number below.

1. **One synthetic organisation, one synthetic grant.** Halewater Commons Trust does not
   exist; neither does the Wren Hill Foundation. See `ladder/fixture-README.md` §1 and §6.
   Every effect here is an effect *for this applicant on this call*. n = 1.
2. **An offline replay, not the deployed pipeline.** Documents were generated by a
   reconstruction of the stage sequence, not by worker v26 running against production.
3. **`reasoning_effort: "low"` on every critic call**, pinned so the replayed rungs and the
   new rungs stay comparable. A more deliberate critic might rank differently. Nothing here
   tells you whether it would.
4. **Four documents per rung, one call per critic per rung.** No repeats, so **critic variance
   is entirely unmeasured**. A ranking that changes between two runs of the same call would be
   invisible to this design.
5. **Six of ten critic cells are MISSING**, and they are not missing at random — see §1. The
   entire count axis rests on **one critic** (`x-ai/grok-4.6`), at **two of four rungs**.
6. **Four rungs cannot establish a dose–response curve.** They can show a direction or fail to.
   This run fails to.
7. The fixture's twelve facts are clean, consistent and mutually non-contradictory. A real
   crawl is not. An effect that needs a tidy ledger will not survive the crawler.

That is the entire curve. **Two points. One critic. Quadrupling referent supply from 6 to 12

I will not dress that up as a finding — with n = 1 document per cell and no repeat calls, a

referents, not twelve.** That is the only quantitative guidance this run produced, and it is
guidance about *diminishing returns in the document*, not about fundability, which was not
measured at 9 or at 3.

This is the most decision-relevant comparison in the run, and the only one where a like-for-like
control exists: **the same six referent names**, the same critic, the same model, the same

**The ranking is identical. The funded document is identical. The machine-generated verdict is

not lose to count. Both axes are null.

And critic_a, on the thin arm alone, called all four documents machine-generated and all four

## 5. The referent-weight question, re-opened

### 5a. What the previous τ was actually computed against

`worker/referent_weight.ts` lines 47–61 report:

> Kendall τ-b against the blind ranking, n = 4 — referent count 0.913, counterparty 0.707,
> weighted mentions 0.333, load-bearing 0.183, **document weight 0.000**

and calls that ordering "the only fundability ordering available".

Traced to source (`scratchpad/qloop/unblocked/referent-weight.md` §3), it was computed against
**the blind *ranking* on the `evidence-poor` case — B > D > C > A — n = 4, six pairs, one case,
from a different experiment entirely.** Not against the ladder, and not against fundability
verdicts: a preference ordering, described in the module as a fundability ordering.

The task brief offered two hypotheses. Neither is right, and the truth is a third thing:

- It was **not** the iteration-1 verdicts (15 not-fundable to 1 fundable, nearly all ties), so
  the "τ against an all-ties ordering" objection does not apply — the ordering was strict.
- It was **not** derived from the ladder rungs, so it is not tautological in the way feared.
- But the note **already recorded its own confound** and the module header dropped it: on that
  case the referent counts were 3/2/2/1, "nearly a perfect proxy for rank". Count was handed a
  baseline of 0.913 by an accident of that one case. The comparison was, in the note's own
  word, "rigged".

So the headline number in the module is real arithmetic on one case of four documents, carrying
a confound its own source document names and its own header comment omits.

### 5b. Recomputed against the real verdicts

Predictors computed deterministically over the documents with `worker/referent_weight.ts`,
using the fixture's audited referent list. Blind rank and fundability are the critics' own.
Pooled Kendall τ-b over **within-case pairs only** — 4 cases × 6 pairs = 24 pairs. Pairs are
never compared across rungs, because the predictor scales differ between rungs.

| predictor | τ-b vs blind rank | τ-b vs fundable | ties in 24 pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **critic's own referent count** (a model output, not deterministic) | **+0.577** | +0.370 | 6 |
| load-bearing referents | **+0.298** | +0.211 | 1 |
| answer word count | +0.167 | +0.218 | 0 |
| **referents present — the count the gate would score** | **+0.167** | +0.182 | **18** |
| counterparty referents | +0.109 | −0.111 | 10 |
| **document weight — the measure that was built** | **−0.333** | −0.327 | 0 |
| weighted mentions | −0.417 | −0.436 | 0 |

Read this carefully, because it does not say what either side hoped.

1. **Deterministic referent count ties in 18 of 24 pairs.** On a controlled fixture the four
   arms of a rung all use nearly every referent offered, so presence cannot distinguish them.
   Its τ of +0.167 is the residue of six informative pairs. **Count is not predictive here; it
   is nearly silent.** Its earlier 0.913 was an artefact of one case where supply happened to
   vary between documents.
2. **Document weight is worse than chance**, at −0.333, and weighted mentions worse still at
   −0.417. Both are volume aggregates and both reward the arm that repeats names most — arm C,
   which is third in every ranking. The earlier verdict "weight is at chance" was generous;
   against real verdicts it is actively wrong-signed. **The module's own conclusion — do not
   move `sufficiency.ts` `activeScorer()` off `countScorer` — stands, and is now better
   supported than when it was written.**
3. **The best deterministic predictor is load-bearing referents, at +0.298**, and it is the one
   sub-signal that asks whether the name does work rather than how often it appears. It is
   modest, it comes from 24 pairs in one fixture, and it is not fit to be a threshold.
4. **The best predictor of all is the critic's own count, at +0.577** — a model output, which
   the project's own rules forbid trusting for counting, and which is endogenous (a critic that
   likes a document has already decided to read its names generously).

**Which should the sufficiency gate score? On this evidence: neither, yet.** Count is
near-silent, weight is wrong-signed, and load-bearing is the only candidate pointing the right
way. Keep `countScorer` — not because count was vindicated, but because nothing has earned the
right to replace it, and swapping in a wrong-signed measure would be worse than keeping a
silent one. `SUFFICIENCY_THRESHOLD.ladderStatus` should stay `"pending"`.

**And the category error named in the source note still stands and is the more important
point:** the sufficiency gate scores the *pre-payment ledger*; referent weight scores a
*finished narrative*. Weight cannot be read from an intake form because no document exists yet.
Even a validated weight measure belongs in `validate`, not in the pre-payment gate.

## 6. The one-system question

All four obtained verdicts answered "one system producing four outputs". **This measures
nothing, and the instrument is the reason.**

The packet's question 5 reads:

> do these four read as four different applicants, or as one system producing four outputs?

It is a forced binary offering two options, one of which is the experimenter's hypothesis. The
earlier instrument test already showed a 4/4 "one system" result was an artefact of a leading
prompt, and this prompt has the same defect.

Worse, the answer is **true by construction**. All four documents are written for one synthetic
applicant off one ledger. They necessarily share the organisation name, the town, the ward, the
coordinator, the college, the vendor and every figure in the fixture. And that is exactly what
every critic cited as its evidence — critic_b at n06: *"Same org, same town, same ward, same
hall, same coordinator, same college, same hire firm, same 27/18, same 24/19/11"*; at n12:
*"same two staff names, same college, same YJ team, same hall/boathouse/caterer/minibus"*; at
n06thin: *"Same legal facts, same Marlpit / St Aidan's / Dunmore / Northgate / Priya / Second
Chances / £214k / 4.2 FTE stack in every Q1"*.

Every one of those is a **fixture constant**, not a stylistic fingerprint. The question cannot
separate "the pipeline has a detectable house voice" from "these four documents describe the
same organisation". **Record the 4/4 as uninformative. Do not cite it as evidence of a house
voice.** A test that could answer the question needs four *different* applicants.

## 11. What follows

**Say it plainly, because the data says it plainly.** The 2026-08-26 decision was to expand the
intake into an automated evidence interview and crawl the applicant's domain in full, on the
theory that the pipeline's quality problem is data starvation. This run was built to test that
theory and it **did not find the effect**:

- Referent presence in the document is already at ~100% of supply from three referents onward.
  The generator is not starved and never was — it uses everything it is given.
- Argumentative *use* of referents saturates at about nine. Referents 10, 11 and 12 appear and
  do no extra work.
- Quadrupling supply from 6 to 12 did not raise the count of fundable documents.
- Stripping all specificity from six referents, holding the names fixed, changed neither the
  ranking, nor the funded document, nor the machine-generated verdict.
- Across every obtained ranking, the ordering of the arms was set by **generator strength**, not
  by evidence. Arm A is last in 4 of 4; the winner is an opus arm in 4 of 4.

Against that: **six of ten cells are missing, the count axis rests on one critic at two rungs,
and critic variance is unmeasured.** This is weak evidence for a null, and a null on weak
evidence is not proof of no effect. But the burden has shifted. The intake expansion is a large,
customer-facing, pre-payment change, and the single experiment built to justify it came back
empty on both of its axes.

**Recommended, in order:**

1. **Do not build the expanded intake yet.** Finish the measurement first — it is cheap.
2. **Fix the transport, not the budget.** Six paid completions were destroyed by a 60-second MCP
   ceiling. A direct HTTPS call with a longer client timeout, or an MCP server configured above
   60 s, recovers the four missing rungs for roughly $1. Money was never the constraint.
3. **Then measure variance before measuring anything else.** Three repeat calls on one rung, same
   packet, same settings. If the ranking moves between repeats, every number in §3 and §4 is
   noise and should be withdrawn.
4. **Build the deterministic budget-closure check now** (§7e). Two of twenty documents ship
   budgets that do not add up, one of them in the production default arm, and every internal
   validator passed them. This is unambiguous, cheap and independent of everything above.
5. **Fix `ledgerReferents()`'s sentence-initial-label defect and `limitedText()`'s exact-match
   attachment rule** (§7a, §7c). Both are live bugs found by this audit.
6. **Correct the header comment in `worker/referent_weight.ts`**, which cites this report for
   figures this report no longer stands behind.
7. **Keep `activeScorer()` on `countScorer` and `ladderStatus` on `"pending"`.** Not a
   vindication of count — count ties in 18 of 24 pairs and is nearly silent — but document
   weight scores worse than chance and nothing else has earned a threshold.
8. **If the intake work proceeds regardless**, target **nine** referents, not twelve, and spend
   the effort on making referents *load-bearing* rather than *numerous*: load-bearing referents
   are the only deterministic signal in this run pointing the right way (τ +0.298), and raw
   presence is the one the gate currently scores (τ +0.167, tied in three quarters of pairs).

## The new blocker: a 60-second transport ceiling, not money

Calls are accepted, generated and **billed** upstream, then lost to the MCP server's 60s call
ceiling. No generation id is returned on transport timeout, so the paid-for text cannot be
recovered. `timeout_ms` does not help — the server's own cap fires first.

Corroborated across three packets and two model families independently, so it is a harness
property rather than a model, key or content problem. The pattern is systematic:
**every `openai/gpt-5.6-sol` call timed out and most `x-ai/grok-4.6` calls returned.** One
agent's billing arithmetic put a lost grok completion at ~13k tokens at ~50 tok/s, about four
minutes — arithmetically out of reach of a 60-second transport.

**More budget does not fix this.** ~$29 remains, far above the $3 floor. Finishing needs a
transport that can wait out a long generation.

## ERROR 1, mine: a mapping paired with the wrong packet

The workflow script paired **critic_a's packet** with **critic_b's map** for n06, and
`.map-n03-critic_a.txt` with `_msg.txt` for n03. A sub-agent caught the n03 case by byte-matching
document blocks rather than trusting the map it was handed; the n06 case was caught afterwards
by the same method.

For n06 the consequence was not cosmetic. The critic's raw ranking `Doc 1 > Doc 4 > Doc 2 > Doc 3`
decodes, under the map actually used, to:

| | as first reported | CORRECTED |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ranking | D > B > C > A | **B > C > D > A** |
| would fund | D — single prompt + stronger generator | **B — pipeline + stronger generator** |

The first reading was evidence against the pipeline. The corrected reading funds the shipped
default. The critic's judgement never changed; only which document it was looking at.

Verified twice by independent methods — a mid-document probe, then three probes per document at
head, middle and tail, all four documents consistent and non-overlapping. The two rungs whose
judging agents built their own packets (`n12`, `n06thin`) verify clean.

## ERROR 2: per-agent cost figures in this run are not trustworthy

An agent noticed its own credit-meter readings were **identical** to another agent's, overturned
its own published cost figure, and said so. The meter is account-wide and concurrent agents move
it between reads, so a delta is not a per-agent cost. Total spend is sound; per-call attribution
in this run is not. Cost per order therefore remains **UNMEASURED**.

family returned for either.

Referent weight also remains **unevaluated**, for the same reason: the recomputation against real
