Why this exists

Everyone is writing proposals with the same chatbot now.

Which means the same reviewers keep opening the same application, in the same voice, with the same plan. Three things happen, and none of them are good for you.

01

They can tell

Someone who has read four hundred applications spots generated writing in a paragraph. What it signals is that nobody at your organisation cared enough to write it themselves, which is a strange message to send while asking for a quarter of a million dollars.

02

It misses the points

Every donor gives your application a mark, and the marks sit in specific places. A smooth proposal that ignores where they sit loses to a plainer one that hits them squarely.

03

It collides with your neighbours

The circle of organisations chasing any one grant is smaller than it looks, and many of them are typing into the same tool. When two applications land on one desk looking like siblings, the reviewer notices, and it goes badly for both. This is the one that ends organisations, and it is the reason the next section exists.

No copies

One organisation per idea. Enforced, not promised.

Your approach is written down and reserved to you before a single sentence is drafted. From that moment, nobody else applying to the same grant can be given it. Not steered away from it. Given it is not possible. Approaches are reserved in the order organisations arrive, so there is a good reason to start early.

No two ways about it

Four locks, and none of them are a polite request to the AI.

Asking a model nicely to "be different" is not a guarantee, it is a hope. So we do not do that. Your approach is reserved in a database before writing starts, and the finished text is checked against the others before anything reaches you.

LOCK ONE

Your approach is written down first

Before any writing, your project is recorded plainly: what the activity is, who it serves, where, and how it is delivered. That record is filed against the grant and reserved to your organisation.

LOCK TWO

A repeat is refused outright

A second organisation asking for the same combination on the same grant is rejected by the system and has to be planned differently. It is not a preference that somebody can override.

LOCK THREE

Different shape, different opening

The structure and the first paragraph are reserved the same way, so two proposals for one grant cannot be built the same way or begin the same way even when the subject is close.

LOCK FOUR

The finished text is checked, and can fail

Every draft is compared with every other proposal we have written for that grant. Too close and it is automatically rewritten and re-checked until it is genuinely its own. Nothing that fails the check is ever delivered.

But what if someone already took my idea?

Far less likely than it sounds. A grant this size has thousands of workable combinations once you account for the activity, who it serves, where, and how it is delivered. More to the point, your proposal is built out of your own partners, your own communities and your own track record, so your strongest approach is usually one no other organisation could credibly claim anyway. In the rare case where the obvious approach is already taken, we show you what is open before you pay a thing.

What we will not claim

We cannot speak for the other applicants who never came near us, and we will not pretend otherwise. Our promise covers every proposal written here, and every package arrives with its own check so you can see it rather than take our word for it. Your writing is never shown to another organisation, and what we keep on file is only the shape of an approach, never anybody's text.

Questions

Straight answers.

You work with organisations competing for the same money. Is that not a conflict?

Two organisations going for the same grant can both be customers here. That is exactly why the locks exist and why they run on their own rather than depending on someone remembering to check. Your writing is never shown to anybody, and the only thing we keep in a shared record is the shape of your approach, which is what lets us refuse it to the next person.

Is there a limit on how many organisations you will work with on one grant?

No. We never turn you away because a grant is busy. Approaches are reserved in the order organisations arrive, and since the proposal is built from your own partners, communities and history, the approach that suits you is rarely one somebody else could have taken. Arriving early does help, so if you know which grant you are going for, start before the week of the deadline rather than during it.

Will it make things up about my organisation?

No. It will not produce claims about your track record, your staff, your partners or your finances that you have not given it. Where something is missing the draft says so plainly instead of papering over it, so you can fill it in or take it out. A funded proposal built on an invented claim is a serious problem for you, and we are not going to create one.

Do I need old proposals to upload?

No, it is optional, but it is the single biggest difference in what comes out. With one to three old documents it sounds like your organisation. Without them it sounds like a capable stranger. If you have nothing, you are given a house voice chosen so it is not the one somebody else applying to the same grant received.

Which donors does this work for?

Any of them. Embassies, EU institutions, UN agencies, development agencies, private foundations — the method is the same everywhere, because it works from the donor's own document: what they ask for, what they require, and how they describe their decision. Where a donor publishes evaluation criteria, we check your proposal against those exact criteria. Where they publish none, we say so plainly rather than inventing a standard.

How do I pay?

By card, through Stripe's secure checkout, in dollars. Stablecoin on request. You get a receipt your finance officer can file, and if the eligibility check finds your organisation cannot apply to that grant, the payment comes back automatically in full. Payment comes before work starts.

How long does it take?

Most proposals are ready within 5 to 30 minutes, depending on the length and complexity of the grant, how many documents your package includes, and how much material you give us to work with. You watch every step happen live on your order page, and we email you the moment everything is ready — so you can close the page and get on with your day.

What happens to my data?

Your uploads and drafts are yours. We keep them so you can reuse your voice next time, and we delete them the day you ask. Nothing you write is ever used to write somebody else's proposal.

Will this get me the grant?

Nobody can promise that, and be careful with anyone who does. What this does is make sure you send something complete, correct, checked against what the donor actually asks for, written like a person, and unlike anything else we have written for that grant. That part is inside your control. The decision is the panel's.
